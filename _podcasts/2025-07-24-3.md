## Prompt

Am 2025-07-22, <https://notebooklm.google.com/notebook/24f7c825-2e94-4a63-ba90-5acda4e49035>

> Discuss the development of rabbinic Judaism under Islamic hegemony on a level suitable for first-year university students.


> First, explain who the Geonim are and how the emerged as leaders of the rabbinic community. What kind of functions did they have and what sources did they leave behind? 


> Second, explain how gaonic leadership is to be understood in the context of Islamic rule. In what ways did the Geonim respond to the legal, political, and theological challenges of Islam? How were the concepts of leadership, law, and oral tradition interwoven with Islamic ideas? What is Judeo-Arabic and why was it used? Use Saadiah Gaon's Tafsir as an example of a response to Islam and use of Judeo-Arabic.


> Third, who were the Qaraites (also spelled Karaites)? When do we first have evidence of them? What are the theories about their origin -- to what extent their movement grew out of inner-Jewish concerns or in response to Islam? Did they have legal or political structures that defined their community seperately from the rabbanites? How did they respond to Islamic legal and theological challenges, perhaps differently from the rabbinic community? Besides the Qaraites, were there other non-rabbinic Jewish groups under Islamic rule?


> Throughout the overview, explain how researchers' opinions have evolved on these questions.


> Do not invent any information even if it is asked in this prompt. Instead leave it out if it's not in the sources provided here.


> Make sure to allow for some uncertainty in the interpretation or dating of primary sources. Make sure to attribute information to specific primary sources and attribute the views of researchers to the people who hold these views. Use the correct name and correct pronunciation of each author.


> Don't end with a question. End with a summary of the development of rabbinic Judaism (over against its rivals) in the Islamic period.


> The articles are in English and German. Use the source "Pronunciation Guide" to determine the correct pronunciation of words that are not English or German. If a word does not appear in the pronunciation guide, determine from context which language it is (most likely Arabic, Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, or Persian) and pronounce it accordingly.
